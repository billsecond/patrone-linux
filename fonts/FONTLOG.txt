FONTLOG for Patrone Sans
========================

This file records how Patrone Sans differs from the font it comes from, as
the SIL Open Font License's FAQ asks of a derivative font.


Basic font information
----------------------

Patrone Sans is the interface font of PatroneOS. It is Noto Sans,
version 2.015 (the NotoSans-v2.015 tag of
https://github.com/notofonts/latin-greek-cyrillic, commit c4a321e1), with
a few changes that make letters and figures easier to tell apart on screen.
It keeps Noto Sans's look, spacing, kerning, vertical metrics, features and
language support (Latin, Greek and Cyrillic).

It comes as two variable fonts with one axis, weight, from 100 (Thin) to
900 (Black), with named instances at every hundred:

    PatroneSans[wght].ttf          upright
    PatroneSans-Italic[wght].ttf   italic

Noto Sans was designed by the Monotype design team and Irene Vlachou.
"Noto" is a trademark of Google LLC; Patrone Sans has a name of its own,
as a modified version should.

License: SIL Open Font License, Version 1.1 (OFL.txt). Noto Sans's
license names no Reserved Font Name.


ChangeLog
---------

2026-10-26 (William Daugherty) Patrone Sans 1.000

- l has a tail. In Noto Sans, l, capital I and the vertical bar are all
  plain upright strokes, and 1 differs only by its small flag, so "Il1|"
  and words like "Illinois" can be misread, worst of all in passwords,
  keys and file names, where no context helps. A tailed l is the usual
  answer in fonts drawn for legibility: DIN 1450, the German standard on
  legible lettering, asks that easily confused characters such as I, l
  and 1 be made distinct, and Source Sans, Fira Sans, IBM Plex Sans and
  Atkinson Hyperlegible all give l a tail. The tail is Noto Sans's own: it is
  the foot of Noto Sans's t, taken from each master, set on l's own stem
  and made 62% as long, since l has no crossbar to balance it. l is wider
  by its tail (258 to 290 units in the regular weight), and glyphs made
  from l (ľ, ļ, ŀ, ĺ, ł, the ﬂ ligature, the lj digraph and the like) are
  wider by the same amount. Capital I stays as it was: with l tailed, the
  three are distinct, and serifs on I would change the font's look the
  most of any change, in every capitalized word.

- 0 has a slash. Noto Sans's zero is only a little narrower than its O,
  which is not enough at small sizes, in serial numbers, codes and times.
  The slash is the one Noto Sans already has for its slashed zero (the
  "zero" feature); it is now the default, for the tabular figures (the
  default ones) and the proportional ones. The slash stays inside the
  zero's outline, so it isn't mistaken for Ø, whose stroke crosses it.
  Oldstyle figures, superscripts, subscripts and fractions keep the plain
  zero: they're small, and a slash in them is more smudge than help.

- One axis, weight. Noto Sans's width axis (condensed to normal) is left
  out: the desktop uses normal width, and leaving it out makes the font
  smaller. The weights the desktop uses (regular, medium, semibold and
  bold) are all there, where Ubuntu's Noto Sans package has only regular
  and bold.

- Names and notices: the family is Patrone Sans, the vendor ID PTRN, the
  version 1.000; the copyright keeps Noto's notice and adds one for these
  changes, and the license fields name the OFL 1.1. Noto's trademark
  notice is gone, since the font no longer has Noto's name.

- Finishing: a gasp table that asks for smoothing at every size, and no
  TrueType instructions. GNOME's default font hinting ("slight") makes
  FreeType hint every TrueType font with its own autohinter, ignoring the
  font's instructions, so Noto Sans's (made by ttfautohint) do nothing
  there; without instructions, FreeType's autohinter also hints Patrone
  Sans at the "full" setting, where a font with instructions would be left
  to them. (Checked on Ubuntu 26.04: Ubuntu's Noto Sans and a copy of it
  with its instructions removed draw the same pixels at "slight", and
  different ones at "full".) The Macintosh copies of some feature names
  are dropped.

Kept as they are, on purpose: everything else. The x-height (536 of 1000
units), the open apertures (c, e, s, a) and the spacing were drawn for
screen text and are already what a UI font wants at 9 to 11 points. The
vertical metrics (ascender 1069, descender 293, no line gap) are exactly
Noto Sans's, so switching between the two fonts doesn't move anything on
screen. Default figures were already tabular (all ten one width, so
numbers that change, like a clock or a progress count, don't shift), with
proportional figures in the "pnum" feature.
